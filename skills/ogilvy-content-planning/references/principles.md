# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-005 · Chapter 2 · DO YOUR HOMEWORK

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Investigate how buyers think, their language, valued attributes and motivating promises.
Adaptation: Use supplied interviews or authorized research; mark untested buyer assumptions.
Limit: Do not fabricate customer quotes or reduce all motivations to pain points.

## OG-038 · Chapter 7 · BODY COPY

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Address a person in everyday language and explain specifically what the product can do for them.
Adaptation: Rewrite vague copy into clear task, benefit and mechanism using verified facts.
Limit: Simple wording is not enough if it remains generic; technical detail is allowed when useful to the reader.

## OG-041 · Chapter 7 · SHORT COPY OR LONG?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: He argues that pertinent detail and well-written long copy can sell; the opening must earn attention.
Adaptation: Choose length according to the question and decision, not a universal brevity or verbosity rule.
Limit: Preserve his distinction between experience, reported tests and unsupported personal belief; do not claim universal current digital superiority.

## OG-046 · Chapter 9 · Selected corporate-copy block

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Interchangeable corporate slogans communicate little about a specific company.
Adaptation: Flag sentences that could fit almost any competitor and replace them with grounded detail.
Limit: Brand tone is not required to be purely rational or functional.

## OG-048 · Chapter 10 · Selected desire and obstacle paragraphs

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author combines attractive destination imagery with practical how-to information and addresses cost and fear as obstacles.
Adaptation: For a service, connect desire with concrete adoption information and known uncertainties.
Limit: The application to software adoption is an analogy we propose; do not carry over national stereotypes or pretend obstacles are verified without research.

## OG-049 · Chapter 11 · PRINT

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: In B2B, advertising can prepare the sale and generate leads rather than close it alone.
Adaptation: Map the next appropriate step from a communication to inquiry and conversation.
Limit: Not every helpful blog post needs an aggressive CTA.

## OG-062 · Chapter 13 · Advertising for good causes

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Awareness advertising may prepare the audience for subsequent personal fundraising rather than yield immediate donations.
Adaptation: Match measurement to the funnel stage for causes and public-interest efforts.
Limit: Do not claim awareness alone caused downstream donations or behavioral change.

## OG-063 · Chapter 13 · Cancer in India

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Campaigns can aim first to shift understanding and attitudes, then invite a specific beneficial action.
Adaptation: Design measurable stages when the goal is education, attitude or participation rather than a sale.
Limit: Historical outcome in one campaign is not a forecast; follow ethical safeguards.

## OG-065 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: P&G is described as promising a major benefit and explicitly confirming it in demonstrations and messages.
Adaptation: Choose a principal credible promise and provide matching proof at relevant touchpoints.
Limit: Not a universal ban on multiple benefits; the chapter notes concurrent campaigns and exceptions.

## OG-079 · Chapter 16 · Heavy users

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A minority of category users may account for much volume and differ in motivations from occasional users.
Adaptation: Investigate usage frequency, needs, margins and reachable segments rather than one average persona.
Limit: Do not assume the historical 80/20 examples apply; category heavy use is not necessarily brand loyalty or profit.

## OG-080 · Chapter 16 · Repertory of brands

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author reports that some buyers rotate among familiar brands; reinforcement may matter more than wholesale conversion.
Adaptation: Ask about consideration sets, repeat use and reasons to return.
Limit: Category- and era-dependent theory, not a universal claim about modern software purchasers.

## OG-083 · Chapter 18 · Bill Bernbach

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Bernbach valued originality and integrated copy/art, while insisting the product remains the hero; he criticized research.
Adaptation: Seek creative execution relevant to the product, and test when evidence is needed without assuming every hybrid is best.
Limit: Do not claim Bernbach would always choose emotional over informative messaging.

## OG-084 · Chapter 18 · Leo Burnett

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Burnett encouraged discovering inherent product drama and natural language, not difference for its own sake.
Adaptation: Look for specific product truth and an engaging human presentation.
Limit: This is a creative approach, not evidence that any particular slogan will sell.

## OG-087 · Chapter 19 · Not enough information

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author criticizes slogans that omit useful product information and favors factual specificity.
Adaptation: State scope, mechanics, relevant limits and verifiable value, avoiding empty superiority promises.
Limit: Important facts depend on audience and medium; neither longer copy nor facts alone guarantee outcomes.
