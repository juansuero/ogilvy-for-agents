# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-007 · Chapter 2 · BRAND IMAGE

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Brand personality draws on name, presentation, price, advertising style and the product itself.
Adaptation: Review the promised image alongside delivery and the experience, not just the logo.
Limit: An emotional or identity-based route is allowed; evidence discipline must not flatten all communication into technical specifications.

## OG-009 · Chapter 2 · WHAT’S THE BIG IDEA?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author evaluates ideas for surprise, originality, strategic fit and long-lasting potential; preparation supplies relevant material.
Adaptation: Develop distinct creative concepts and explain tradeoffs; do not certify a concept as a guaranteed big idea.
Limit: The thirty-year question is a heuristic about endurance, not a factual forecast or pass/fail requirement.

## OG-010 · Chapter 2 · MAKE THE PRODUCT THE HERO / WHAT ABOUT SEX?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The product should be central, and attention-getting elements should be relevant to it.
Adaptation: Check whether a visual, story or joke advances the intended product meaning.
Limit: Relevant creativity is not forbidden just because it is not literal.

## OG-037 · Chapter 7 · ILLUSTRATIONS / WARNING

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The idea and subject matter, curiosity, visible outcomes and audience relevance matter; pictures may be misunderstood.
Adaptation: Describe the intended takeaway and plausible misreadings; validate rather than assume audience interpretation.
Limit: No treating personal aesthetic taste, historic gender assumptions or claimed universal photo superiority as proof.

## OG-065 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: P&G is described as promising a major benefit and explicitly confirming it in demonstrations and messages.
Adaptation: Choose a principal credible promise and provide matching proof at relevant touchpoints.
Limit: Not a universal ban on multiple benefits; the chapter notes concurrent campaigns and exceptions.

## OG-067 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source argues research can compare the importance, uniqueness and persuasiveness of promises.
Adaptation: Evaluate promises with buyer evidence and outcome-oriented tests; distinguish reported preference from observed behavior.
Limit: Importance ratings and split-run sample requests are different methods with different outputs.

## OG-069 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy distrusts recall as a proxy for purchasing effects; he prefers tests of changed brand preference for pretesting TV ads.
Adaptation: Separate impressions, recall, comprehension, preference, inquiry, sale, profit and retention.
Limit: Preference is still not actual sales; historical correlations must not be universalized.

## OG-073 · Chapter 16 · New products

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Products can fail by being too unfamiliar or, more often per author, lacking a perceptible valuable difference; familiarity can anchor novelty.
Adaptation: Describe a recognizable existing job and the meaningful new customer benefit.
Limit: Different technology alone does not prove a buyer-valued improvement; the historical failure ratio is not a current forecast.

## OG-082 · Chapter 18 · Claude Hopkins

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Hopkins emphasized sales and experimentation, though Ogilvy notes blurred boundaries between tested findings and general observations.
Adaptation: Separate empirical tests from the author’s inferences, and keep tests tied to selling outcomes.
Limit: Do not attribute modern rigorous experimentation to an historical anecdote by default.

## OG-083 · Chapter 18 · Bill Bernbach

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Bernbach valued originality and integrated copy/art, while insisting the product remains the hero; he criticized research.
Adaptation: Seek creative execution relevant to the product, and test when evidence is needed without assuming every hybrid is best.
Limit: Do not claim Bernbach would always choose emotional over informative messaging.

## OG-084 · Chapter 18 · Leo Burnett

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Burnett encouraged discovering inherent product drama and natural language, not difference for its own sake.
Adaptation: Look for specific product truth and an engaging human presentation.
Limit: This is a creative approach, not evidence that any particular slogan will sell.

## OG-085 · Chapter 18 · Raymond Rubicam

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Rubicam linked readership research with creative execution and emphasized behaving properly toward the public.
Adaptation: Use research to enrich creative ideas and retain an ethical credibility check.
Limit: Readership is not equivalent to sales; historical anecdotes do not license misleading claims.
