# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-056 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author emphasizes price, payment terms, premiums and mailing format as important variables to test.
Adaptation: Prioritize high-leverage offer variables before minor headline or color tweaks when relevant.
Limit: Priority depends on a particular market; historical ranking is not a universal causal law.

## OG-057 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Use a profitable mailing as a control and test challengers against it, considering incremental cost and profit.
Adaptation: Keep the current winner in the comparison, define replacement thresholds and account for economics.
Limit: Do not discard proven control due to novelty; confirm that control remains effective today.

## OG-058 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Promotions can increase initial response without necessarily acquiring long-term customers.
Adaptation: Compare retention, quality and profit rather than assuming leads and discounts are equally valuable.
Limit: Do not promise customer lifetime value from short-term results.

## OG-059 · Chapter 12 · Where to advertise

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source highlights comparing inquiries and orders by placement, not merely reach.
Adaptation: Evaluate meaningful conversion and commercial outcome, with spend and follow-up.
Limit: Historic direct-mail measurement is not guaranteed modern causal attribution.

## OG-067 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source argues research can compare the importance, uniqueness and persuasiveness of promises.
Adaptation: Evaluate promises with buyer evidence and outcome-oriented tests; distinguish reported preference from observed behavior.
Limit: Importance ratings and split-run sample requests are different methods with different outputs.

## OG-072 · Chapter 15 · Where I come out

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Research should inform a decision rather than rationalize a conclusion already chosen.
Adaptation: Record the hypothesis and alternative interpretations before interpreting a result.
Limit: The book also admits limitations: long-term campaign value and optimum price remained unresolved for the author.

## OG-073 · Chapter 16 · New products

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Products can fail by being too unfamiliar or, more often per author, lacking a perceptible valuable difference; familiarity can anchor novelty.
Adaptation: Describe a recognizable existing job and the meaningful new customer benefit.
Limit: Different technology alone does not prove a buyer-valued improvement; the historical failure ratio is not a current forecast.

## OG-077 · Chapter 16 · Promotions

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Repeated short-term price promotions can displace brand building and have temporary effects; some promotions such as sampling remain useful.
Adaptation: Assess incremental profit, retention, brand effects and opportunity cost of incentives.
Limit: Ogilvy is not categorically against promotions; do not presume all discounts damage a brand.

## OG-078 · Chapter 16 · Pricing is guesswork

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Higher price may influence desirability even when objective quality is not higher.
Adaptation: Treat price as an economic and perceptual variable; test decisions with project evidence.
Limit: High prices do not prove product quality or guarantee higher sales or profit.

## OG-079 · Chapter 16 · Heavy users

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A minority of category users may account for much volume and differ in motivations from occasional users.
Adaptation: Investigate usage frequency, needs, margins and reachable segments rather than one average persona.
Limit: Do not assume the historical 80/20 examples apply; category heavy use is not necessarily brand loyalty or profit.

## OG-087 · Chapter 19 · Not enough information

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author criticizes slogans that omit useful product information and favors factual specificity.
Adaptation: State scope, mechanics, relevant limits and verifiable value, avoiding empty superiority promises.
Limit: Important facts depend on audience and medium; neither longer copy nor facts alone guarantee outcomes.

## OG-088 · Chapter 19 · What’s wrong with advertising?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The book defends advertising but raises concerns about deception, political marketing and media clutter.
Adaptation: Avoid unsupported guarantees, misleading urgency, fabricated endorsements and unjustified assumptions.
Limit: Do not treat the author’s dated statements about regulation or politics as current legal guidance.
