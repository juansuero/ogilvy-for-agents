# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-048 · Chapter 10 · Selected desire and obstacle paragraphs

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author combines attractive destination imagery with practical how-to information and addresses cost and fear as obstacles.
Adaptation: For a service, connect desire with concrete adoption information and known uncertainties.
Limit: The application to software adoption is an analogy we propose; do not carry over national stereotypes or pretend obstacles are verified without research.

## OG-049 · Chapter 11 · PRINT

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: In B2B, advertising can prepare the sale and generate leads rather than close it alone.
Adaptation: Map the next appropriate step from a communication to inquiry and conversation.
Limit: Not every helpful blog post needs an aggressive CTA.

## OG-052 · Chapter 11 · HOW TO STIMULATE INQUIRIES

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source makes the offer and means to respond visible and easy to use.
Adaptation: Design an accessible next step and explain what happens after the inquiry.
Limit: Digital forms and contact choices are adaptations; do not imply phone/coupon-era research proves a modern conversion uplift.

## OG-053 · Chapter 11 · ANALYSE YOUR INQUIRIES

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Study inquirer intent, follow-up outcomes and the opportunity type, and relate inquiries to their source.
Adaptation: Analyze observed quality and outcome by channel, flag missing data and avoid choosing by volume alone.
Limit: Further margin, cohort and causal analysis is an explicit implementation extension, not a claim that Ogilvy supplies those methods.

## OG-055 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Direct-response channels permit tracing responses and sales, supporting controlled improvements.
Adaptation: Distinguish measured action, acquisition, profit and long-term value; choose channel-specific measures.
Limit: Attribution can be incomplete in cross-channel journeys; do not say every test isolates a single cause.

## OG-056 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author emphasizes price, payment terms, premiums and mailing format as important variables to test.
Adaptation: Prioritize high-leverage offer variables before minor headline or color tweaks when relevant.
Limit: Priority depends on a particular market; historical ranking is not a universal causal law.

## OG-057 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Use a profitable mailing as a control and test challengers against it, considering incremental cost and profit.
Adaptation: Keep the current winner in the comparison, define replacement thresholds and account for economics.
Limit: Do not discard proven control due to novelty; confirm that control remains effective today.

## OG-058 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Promotions can increase initial response without necessarily acquiring long-term customers.
Adaptation: Compare retention, quality and profit rather than assuming leads and discounts are equally valuable.
Limit: Do not promise customer lifetime value from short-term results.

## OG-059 · Chapter 12 · Where to advertise

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source highlights comparing inquiries and orders by placement, not merely reach.
Adaptation: Evaluate meaningful conversion and commercial outcome, with spend and follow-up.
Limit: Historic direct-mail measurement is not guaranteed modern causal attribution.

## OG-061 · Chapter 12 · Television

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author cautions against assuming a larger audience creates more orders.
Adaptation: Measure qualified responses, conversions and profit, not only impressions and clicks.
Limit: Source statement concerns historical TV direct response and is not a general zero-correlation law.

## OG-062 · Chapter 13 · Advertising for good causes

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Awareness advertising may prepare the audience for subsequent personal fundraising rather than yield immediate donations.
Adaptation: Match measurement to the funnel stage for causes and public-interest efforts.
Limit: Do not claim awareness alone caused downstream donations or behavioral change.

## OG-074 · Chapter 16 · Sleeping beauties

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A well-selling but underadvertised product might gain more profit through advertising, but tests may also show no response.
Adaptation: Test incremental profit before assuming the offering needs no communication or guaranteed extra spending.
Limit: Organic success does not establish advertising effectiveness.

## OG-075 · Chapter 16 · Don’t waste time on problem babies

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author advises investing in winners rather than endlessly trying to revive failing products.
Adaptation: Compare prospective return, evidence, strategic constraints and opportunity costs.
Limit: Do not automatically abandon a product on one weak result or ignore necessary product fixes.

## OG-077 · Chapter 16 · Promotions

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Repeated short-term price promotions can displace brand building and have temporary effects; some promotions such as sampling remain useful.
Adaptation: Assess incremental profit, retention, brand effects and opportunity cost of incentives.
Limit: Ogilvy is not categorically against promotions; do not presume all discounts damage a brand.

## OG-079 · Chapter 16 · Heavy users

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A minority of category users may account for much volume and differ in motivations from occasional users.
Adaptation: Investigate usage frequency, needs, margins and reachable segments rather than one average persona.
Limit: Do not assume the historical 80/20 examples apply; category heavy use is not necessarily brand loyalty or profit.

## OG-080 · Chapter 16 · Repertory of brands

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author reports that some buyers rotate among familiar brands; reinforcement may matter more than wholesale conversion.
Adaptation: Ask about consideration sets, repeat use and reasons to return.
Limit: Category- and era-dependent theory, not a universal claim about modern software purchasers.
