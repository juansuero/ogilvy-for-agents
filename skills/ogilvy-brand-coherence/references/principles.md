# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-007 · Chapter 2 · BRAND IMAGE

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Brand personality draws on name, presentation, price, advertising style and the product itself.
Adaptation: Review the promised image alongside delivery and the experience, not just the logo.
Limit: An emotional or identity-based route is allowed; evidence discipline must not flatten all communication into technical specifications.

## OG-046 · Chapter 9 · Selected corporate-copy block

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Interchangeable corporate slogans communicate little about a specific company.
Adaptation: Flag sentences that could fit almost any competitor and replace them with grounded detail.

## OG-065 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: P&G is described as promising a major benefit and explicitly confirming it in demonstrations and messages.
Adaptation: Choose a principal credible promise and provide matching proof at relevant touchpoints.
Limit: Not a universal ban on multiple benefits; the chapter notes concurrent campaigns and exceptions.

## OG-066 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A successful strategy can persist while execution variations continue to be tested.
Adaptation: Separate strategic changes from execution tests; maintain a measured baseline.
Limit: Historical spending, daypart and TV practices are not modern universal benchmarks.

## OG-077 · Chapter 16 · Promotions

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Repeated short-term price promotions can displace brand building and have temporary effects; some promotions such as sampling remain useful.
Adaptation: Assess incremental profit, retention, brand effects and opportunity cost of incentives.
Limit: Ogilvy is not categorically against promotions; do not presume all discounts damage a brand.

## OG-078 · Chapter 16 · Pricing is guesswork

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Higher price may influence desirability even when objective quality is not higher.
Adaptation: Treat price as an economic and perceptual variable; test decisions with project evidence.
Limit: High prices do not prove product quality or guarantee higher sales or profit.

## OG-085 · Chapter 18 · Raymond Rubicam

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Rubicam linked readership research with creative execution and emphasized behaving properly toward the public.
Adaptation: Use research to enrich creative ideas and retain an ethical credibility check.
Limit: Readership is not equivalent to sales; historical anecdotes do not license misleading claims.

## OG-087 · Chapter 19 · Not enough information

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author criticizes slogans that omit useful product information and favors factual specificity.
Adaptation: State scope, mechanics, relevant limits and verifiable value, avoiding empty superiority promises.
Limit: Important facts depend on audience and medium; neither longer copy nor facts alone guarantee outcomes.
