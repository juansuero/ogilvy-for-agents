# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-012 · Chapter 2 · REPEAT YOUR WINNERS

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Keep successful advertising until evidence indicates wear-out rather than replacing it because the team is bored.
Adaptation: Preserve a good existing asset and ask what performance evidence would justify changing it.
Limit: Repeated exposure and digital contexts require project-specific observation; historic print counts do not establish a universal frequency.

## OG-015 · Chapter 2 · PURSUIT OF KNOWLEDGE

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy criticizes testing individual ads without learning across the results.
Adaptation: Store comparable outcomes, conditions and lessons, including unsuccessful attempts.
Limit: No automatic access to past projects or persistent memory is implied.

## OG-016 · Chapter 2 · THE LESSONS OF DIRECT RESPONSE

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: He contrasts general advertising's measurement difficulty with attributable orders in direct response.
Adaptation: Trace observed responses to commercial outcomes and state attribution limitations.
Limit: Attribution in a modern mixed journey is not perfect causal proof; the source does not supply a modern statistical test.

## OG-021 · Chapter 3 · RESEARCHERS

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Research should be intellectually honest, timely, understandable and useful rather than paralyzed by perfectionism.
Adaptation: Distinguish evidence, unknowns and limited conclusions; deliver a useful bounded analysis.
Limit: Urgency does not excuse manufactured certainty or cherry-picking.

## OG-053 · Chapter 11 · ANALYSE YOUR INQUIRIES

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Study inquirer intent, follow-up outcomes and the opportunity type, and relate inquiries to their source.
Adaptation: Analyze observed quality and outcome by channel, flag missing data and avoid choosing by volume alone.
Limit: Further margin, cohort and causal analysis is an explicit implementation extension, not a claim that Ogilvy supplies those methods.

## OG-055 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Direct-response channels permit tracing responses and sales, supporting controlled improvements.
Adaptation: Distinguish measured action, acquisition, profit and long-term value; choose channel-specific measures.
Limit: Attribution can be incomplete in cross-channel journeys; do not say every test isolates a single cause.

## OG-056 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author emphasizes price, payment terms, premiums and mailing format as important variables to test.
Adaptation: Prioritize high-leverage offer variables before minor headline or color tweaks when relevant.
Limit: Priority depends on a particular market; historical ranking is not a universal causal law.

## OG-057 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Use a profitable mailing as a control and test challengers against it, considering incremental cost and profit.
Adaptation: Keep the current winner in the comparison, define replacement thresholds and account for economics.
Limit: Do not discard proven control due to novelty; confirm that control remains effective today.

## OG-058 · Chapter 12 · Direct mail

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Promotions can increase initial response without necessarily acquiring long-term customers.
Adaptation: Compare retention, quality and profit rather than assuming leads and discounts are equally valuable.
Limit: Do not promise customer lifetime value from short-term results.

## OG-065 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: P&G is described as promising a major benefit and explicitly confirming it in demonstrations and messages.
Adaptation: Choose a principal credible promise and provide matching proof at relevant touchpoints.
Limit: Not a universal ban on multiple benefits; the chapter notes concurrent campaigns and exceptions.

## OG-066 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A successful strategy can persist while execution variations continue to be tested.
Adaptation: Separate strategic changes from execution tests; maintain a measured baseline.
Limit: Historical spending, daypart and TV practices are not modern universal benchmarks.

## OG-067 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source argues research can compare the importance, uniqueness and persuasiveness of promises.
Adaptation: Evaluate promises with buyer evidence and outcome-oriented tests; distinguish reported preference from observed behavior.
Limit: Importance ratings and split-run sample requests are different methods with different outputs.

## OG-068 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A split-run test of two ad headlines with different promises used sample requests as its outcome.
Adaptation: Specify assignment, control, target metric and decision criterion; state what the test does and does not measure.
Limit: Requests for samples are not automatically sales or profit; real tests may be confounded.

## OG-069 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy distrusts recall as a proxy for purchasing effects; he prefers tests of changed brand preference for pretesting TV ads.
Adaptation: Separate impressions, recall, comprehension, preference, inquiry, sale, profit and retention.
Limit: Preference is still not actual sales; historical correlations must not be universalized.

## OG-070 · Chapter 15 · Size of sample

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: For time-trend surveys, changes in sample composition or question wording can mimic a trend.
Adaptation: Compare equivalent cohorts and definitions; report sampling limits and uncertainty.
Limit: Not all practical qualitative research requires formal statistical power; do not manufacture significance.

## OG-071 · Chapter 15 · Pitfalls of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Self-report and question framing can bias findings; a stated answer may not reflect action.
Adaptation: Triangulate interviews with observed decisions and test alternative question wording.
Limit: Do not automatically accuse a respondent of dishonesty; multiple biases can explain discrepancies.

## OG-072 · Chapter 15 · Where I come out

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Research should inform a decision rather than rationalize a conclusion already chosen.
Adaptation: Record the hypothesis and alternative interpretations before interpreting a result.
Limit: The book also admits limitations: long-term campaign value and optimum price remained unresolved for the author.

## OG-074 · Chapter 16 · Sleeping beauties

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A well-selling but underadvertised product might gain more profit through advertising, but tests may also show no response.
Adaptation: Test incremental profit before assuming the offering needs no communication or guaranteed extra spending.
Limit: Organic success does not establish advertising effectiveness.

## OG-077 · Chapter 16 · Promotions

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Repeated short-term price promotions can displace brand building and have temporary effects; some promotions such as sampling remain useful.
Adaptation: Assess incremental profit, retention, brand effects and opportunity cost of incentives.
Limit: Ogilvy is not categorically against promotions; do not presume all discounts damage a brand.

## OG-078 · Chapter 16 · Pricing is guesswork

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Higher price may influence desirability even when objective quality is not higher.
Adaptation: Treat price as an economic and perceptual variable; test decisions with project evidence.
Limit: High prices do not prove product quality or guarantee higher sales or profit.

## OG-079 · Chapter 16 · Heavy users

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A minority of category users may account for much volume and differ in motivations from occasional users.
Adaptation: Investigate usage frequency, needs, margins and reachable segments rather than one average persona.
Limit: Do not assume the historical 80/20 examples apply; category heavy use is not necessarily brand loyalty or profit.

## OG-082 · Chapter 18 · Claude Hopkins

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Hopkins emphasized sales and experimentation, though Ogilvy notes blurred boundaries between tested findings and general observations.
Adaptation: Separate empirical tests from the author’s inferences, and keep tests tied to selling outcomes.
Limit: Do not attribute modern rigorous experimentation to an historical anecdote by default.

## OG-083 · Chapter 18 · Bill Bernbach

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Bernbach valued originality and integrated copy/art, while insisting the product remains the hero; he criticized research.
Adaptation: Seek creative execution relevant to the product, and test when evidence is needed without assuming every hybrid is best.
Limit: Do not claim Bernbach would always choose emotional over informative messaging.
