# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-010 · Chapter 2 · MAKE THE PRODUCT THE HERO / WHAT ABOUT SEX?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The product should be central, and attention-getting elements should be relevant to it.
Adaptation: Check whether a visual, story or joke advances the intended product meaning.
Limit: Relevant creativity is not forbidden just because it is not literal.

## OG-037 · Chapter 7 · ILLUSTRATIONS / WARNING

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The idea and subject matter, curiosity, visible outcomes and audience relevance matter; pictures may be misunderstood.
Adaptation: Describe the intended takeaway and plausible misreadings; validate rather than assume audience interpretation.
Limit: No treating personal aesthetic taste, historic gender assumptions or claimed universal photo superiority as proof.

## OG-043 · Chapter 8 · ABOVE AVERAGE: 4, 5, 8

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: These selected formats show performance, connect a familiar problem to a solution, and supply a reason for choosing.
Adaptation: Design a credible demonstration with visible inputs, behavior, outcome and relevant explanation.
Limit: A demo does not prove whole-system reliability; do not transpose television performance rankings to present video channels.

## OG-044 · Chapter 8 · SIXTEEN TIPS: 1, 5, 9, 13

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The selected advice concerns early brand recognition, an engaging relevant opening, matching on-screen words with speech and showing use or outcome.
Adaptation: Plan a clear narrative and avoid contradictory text and voice.
Limit: Exact seconds and repetition counts are contextual; coherent visual explanations need not copy every spoken word.

## OG-045 · Chapter 8 · SIXTEEN TIPS: 15

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author emphasizes how frequently viewers can misunderstand commercials.
Adaptation: Include a comprehension check and disclose whether the asset was actually viewed.
Limit: Do not claim an audience test was conducted when only an expert review was possible.

## OG-054 · Chapter 11 · ADVERTISING TO TOP MANAGEMENT

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Approvers may need broad benefits while specialists need specification detail; separate campaigns can sometimes help.
Adaptation: Identify user, evaluator and approver needs; adapt emphasis while keeping facts consistent.
Limit: Do not assume every company has three people or directors never care about technical details.

## OG-064 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The chapter prioritizes product benefits consumers can actually perceive; advertising cannot rescue a repeatedly disappointing experience.
Adaptation: Audit delivered performance against the claim before buying more traffic.
Limit: Product quality and message are distinct; an improvement must be independently established for that case.

## OG-065 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: P&G is described as promising a major benefit and explicitly confirming it in demonstrations and messages.
Adaptation: Choose a principal credible promise and provide matching proof at relevant touchpoints.
Limit: Not a universal ban on multiple benefits; the chapter notes concurrent campaigns and exceptions.

## OG-068 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A split-run test of two ad headlines with different promises used sample requests as its outcome.
Adaptation: Specify assignment, control, target metric and decision criterion; state what the test does and does not measure.
Limit: Requests for samples are not automatically sales or profit; real tests may be confounded.

## OG-071 · Chapter 15 · Pitfalls of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Self-report and question framing can bias findings; a stated answer may not reflect action.
Adaptation: Triangulate interviews with observed decisions and test alternative question wording.
Limit: Do not automatically accuse a respondent of dishonesty; multiple biases can explain discrepancies.

## OG-086 · Chapter 19 · Can advertising sell bad products?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy argues strong promotion cannot reliably secure repeat purchases for a disappointing product; improving the product is often key.
Adaptation: Diagnose usage, fulfillment and retention before attributing weak sales solely to ads.
Limit: Some short-term sales from promotion remain possible; do not conclude defects without evidence.

## OG-087 · Chapter 19 · Not enough information

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author criticizes slogans that omit useful product information and favors factual specificity.
Adaptation: State scope, mechanics, relevant limits and verifiable value, avoiding empty superiority promises.
Limit: Important facts depend on audience and medium; neither longer copy nor facts alone guarantee outcomes.
