# Coverage and reasoning discipline — v0.2.0

- Selected relevant sections of all 20 chapters are represented; coverage is deliberately selective rather than exhaustive.
- Chapters 12, 14–16 have direct commercial applications; 13 covers public-interest and preparatory communication; 17 cultural adaptation; 18 historical frameworks of creativity and testing; 19 quality, honesty and information; 20 is a historical prediction list, not evidence of the present.
- Claims from the historical book may be anecdotal, category-specific or dated. Do not report old numbers as contemporary benchmarks.
- Distinguish source principle, modern adaptation and client-specific observation before recommending an intervention.
- A persuasive draft does not grant authority to contact third parties, gather private data, publish, or incur costs.
- Avoid rigid universal rules: do not decree that high prices always win, any emotional copy is better, short-term promotions always hurt, users always need manual review, or advertising cannot improve sales of a poorly executed service at all.
- Prefer practical, bounded decisions over an elaborate document when the user asks for one small edit.
