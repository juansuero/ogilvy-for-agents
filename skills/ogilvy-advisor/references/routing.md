# Specialist routing

Choose the single workflow that owns the requested decision. Add at most a second specialist when necessary to address a different, concrete issue. Do not force the general advisor onto narrow asks.

- **ogilvy-acquisition-plan**: Use for choosing a communication objective, offer/next step or assessing a proposed acquisition approach. Not media buying, ad-platform optimization, campaign launching or market demand forecasting.
- **ogilvy-advisor**: Use for an open-ended marketing/communication decision or a request explicitly asking for an Ogilvy-informed integrated review. Do not intercept narrow requests already owned by a specialist; no mandatory preflight on every mention of marketing.
- **ogilvy-brand-coherence**: Use when auditing or defining brand image, message consistency or mismatches between promise and service. Not a full visual identity production service or a compulsory logo redesign.
- **ogilvy-buyer-messaging**: Use when the user, technical evaluator and financial/business approver have different information needs. Not demographic profiling or the assumption that every business has a corporate buying committee.
- **ogilvy-claim-evidence**: Use for numerical claims, comparisons, guarantees, testimonials and credibility audits before drafting or publishing. Not a legal compliance certification or a reason to turn every stylistic statement into a prohibited factual claim.
- **ogilvy-client-communication**: Use for meeting prep, follow-up, explaining a limitation, disagreeing over scope or consolidating feedback. Not autonomous emailing, booking, negotiation commitments or treating every disagreement as a bad client.
- **ogilvy-client-fit**: Use for assessing a prospective account, underpriced future promises or excessive client dependency. Not credit scoring, background checks, legal advice or a universal prohibition on partners/referrals.
- **ogilvy-commercial-proposal**: Use for drafting/reviewing a commercial proposal, quote explanation or scope/pricing tradeoff. Not legal contract review, tax calculation, market-rate advice or a guaranteed ROI model.
- **ogilvy-content-planning**: Use for editorial plans, blog briefs or content audits where the question is relevance, credibility and commercial role. Not technical SEO, ranking prediction, a social algorithm playbook or permission to fabricate subject expertise.
- **ogilvy-copy-workshop**: Use for a specific headline, ad, landing section or commercial text; includes compare/revise modes. Not a whole-site audit by default; not a blog fact-research task or publication authorization.
- **ogilvy-creative-concepts**: Use for campaign concepts, creative routes or reviewing whether a striking idea serves the product. Not a guarantee of virality or a claim to certify a thirty-year big idea.
- **ogilvy-demo-script**: Use for a product/service demo plan, sales-video script or review of a demonstration. Not production/rendering, synthetic customer testimony, or proof that every real case will succeed.
- **ogilvy-experiment-plan**: Use for planning or reviewing a communication test, deciding whether to keep a working asset or framing a measurement question. Not an automated A/B system, significance calculator 
- **ogilvy-inquiry-analysis**: Use for evaluating leads/inquiries, channel comparisons or whether a communication is producing useful commercial outcomes. Not causal attribution certainty from observational data or a substitute for missing cost/sales information.
- **ogilvy-knowledge-review**: Use for documenting a completed test, extracting reusable lessons or reviewing what to preserve/update from prior work. Not automatic cross-client memory sharing, storing data without authorization or a book-summary generator.
- **ogilvy-positioning**: Use to define, compare or audit positioning, differentiation and a main promise. Not a name/logo generator, automatic superiority claim, or substitute for uncollected audience evidence.
- **ogilvy-research-brief**: Use when preparing or auditing the evidence and brief for a website, offer, campaign or service. Not a market-size estimator, automatic competitor scraper, or permission to contact customers.
- **ogilvy-visual-direction**: Use for reviewing a supplied visual or developing a visual brief for a commercial artifact. Not a full technical design-system build or a claim to have generated/rendered an image without tools.
- **ogilvy-website-audit**: Use for commercial/message/visual-hierarchy review of a website or landing page from authorized URL, files or screenshots. Not an SEO, accessibility, security, performance or code-quality certification; these require separate methods and evidence.
- **ogilvy-direct-response**: Use for building, auditing or optimizing a specific direct-response campaign: outreach email, letter, lead-generation message, offer, reply mechanism or structured follow-up. Not an authorization to send messages, obtain personal contact data, scrape prospects or buy ad inventory. For overall channel strategy use ogilvy-acquisition-plan.
- **ogilvy-conversion-diagnosis**: Use to diagnose why a website, campaign or offer attracts attention but underperforms commercially, including gaps between inquiries, sales, experience and retention. Not a full technical analytics setup, statistical causality certification, UX instrument deployment or a justification to alter a product without evidence. For single-source reporting use ogilvy-inquiry-analysis.
- **ogilvy-pricing-promotion**: Use to compare pricing options, positioning signals, trial offers, discounts, bundles, payment terms or promotion-vs-brand investment tradeoffs. Not a market-rate oracle, optimal-price calculator, permission to launch discounts or a guaranteed elasticity model. For client-facing quote documents use ogilvy-commercial-proposal.
- **ogilvy-portfolio-focus**: Use to decide where to allocate bounded marketing time or money among existing offerings, winning campaigns, promising sleepers and underperforming products. Not an automatic instruction to cancel a product, ignore quality issues or optimize only headline sales volume. For a single campaign comparison use ogilvy-experiment-plan.

## Routing precedence and overlap

- Low commercial conversion with plausible product/offer faults → conversion-diagnosis first; inquiry-analysis may quantify exact funnel evidence.
- Comparing existing offerings and budgets → portfolio-focus; do not mistake it for one A/B test.
- Choosing channels/objectives → acquisition-plan; writing a specific response-direct campaign → direct-response.
- A specific pricing or discount decision → pricing-promotion; writing a client quote → commercial-proposal.
- A/B test design → experiment-plan; observed post-campaign reporting → inquiry-analysis; long-term documented learning → knowledge-review.
- Unsupported numerical guarantees → claim-evidence regardless of the final artifact; do not certify claims without evidence.
- Do not automatically use Veltas as the brand. Preserve client context and requested mode.
- For creative choices, distinguish Ogilvy, Hopkins and Bernbach; never imply emotional originality and factual clarity must always be combined.
- Treat chapters 17–20 selectively as insights and historical perspectives, not timeless laws.
