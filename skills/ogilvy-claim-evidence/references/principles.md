# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-011 · Chapter 2 · THE POSITIVELY GOOD

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: reported_theory
Source summary: Raphaelson proposes establishing confidence that a product is good without claiming superiority over competitors.
Adaptation: Prefer concrete strengths and credible reassurance when no comparative evidence exists.
Limit: This is presented as a theory in the source; do not promise its sales outcome.

## OG-021 · Chapter 3 · RESEARCHERS

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Research should be intellectually honest, timely, understandable and useful rather than paralyzed by perfectionism.
Adaptation: Distinguish evidence, unknowns and limited conclusions; deliver a useful bounded analysis.
Limit: Urgency does not excuse manufactured certainty or cherry-picking.

## OG-036 · Chapter 7 · HEADLINES

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author favors benefits, useful information, relevant news, audience signals and specificity; he warns about obscure or blind headlines.
Adaptation: Write alternatives suited to the task and evidence, with brand recognition where context requires it.
Limit: News is conditional; a numeral, brand mention, fixed word count or immediate sales pitch is not compulsory for every digital headline.

## OG-039 · Chapter 7 · BODY COPY

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author contrasts empty self-praise with testimony from users or relevant experts.
Adaptation: Match testimonial content to an authentic, authorized, relevant source.
Limit: Truth/consent checks are our guardrails; the source contains a problematic anonymous-testimonial anecdote, not a modern ethics standard.

## OG-064 · Chapter 14 · Competing with Procter & Gamble

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The chapter prioritizes product benefits consumers can actually perceive; advertising cannot rescue a repeatedly disappointing experience.
Adaptation: Audit delivered performance against the claim before buying more traffic.
Limit: Product quality and message are distinct; an improvement must be independently established for that case.

## OG-067 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The source argues research can compare the importance, uniqueness and persuasiveness of promises.
Adaptation: Evaluate promises with buyer evidence and outcome-oriented tests; distinguish reported preference from observed behavior.
Limit: Importance ratings and split-run sample requests are different methods with different outputs.

## OG-068 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A split-run test of two ad headlines with different promises used sample requests as its outcome.
Adaptation: Specify assignment, control, target metric and decision criterion; state what the test does and does not measure.
Limit: Requests for samples are not automatically sales or profit; real tests may be confounded.

## OG-069 · Chapter 15 · 18 miracles of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy distrusts recall as a proxy for purchasing effects; he prefers tests of changed brand preference for pretesting TV ads.
Adaptation: Separate impressions, recall, comprehension, preference, inquiry, sale, profit and retention.
Limit: Preference is still not actual sales; historical correlations must not be universalized.

## OG-071 · Chapter 15 · Pitfalls of research

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Self-report and question framing can bias findings; a stated answer may not reflect action.
Adaptation: Triangulate interviews with observed decisions and test alternative question wording.
Limit: Do not automatically accuse a respondent of dishonesty; multiple biases can explain discrepancies.

## OG-073 · Chapter 16 · New products

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Products can fail by being too unfamiliar or, more often per author, lacking a perceptible valuable difference; familiarity can anchor novelty.
Adaptation: Describe a recognizable existing job and the meaningful new customer benefit.
Limit: Different technology alone does not prove a buyer-valued improvement; the historical failure ratio is not a current forecast.

## OG-078 · Chapter 16 · Pricing is guesswork

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Higher price may influence desirability even when objective quality is not higher.
Adaptation: Treat price as an economic and perceptual variable; test decisions with project evidence.
Limit: High prices do not prove product quality or guarantee higher sales or profit.

## OG-086 · Chapter 19 · Can advertising sell bad products?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy argues strong promotion cannot reliably secure repeat purchases for a disappointing product; improving the product is often key.
Adaptation: Diagnose usage, fulfillment and retention before attributing weak sales solely to ads.
Limit: Some short-term sales from promotion remain possible; do not conclude defects without evidence.

## OG-087 · Chapter 19 · Not enough information

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author criticizes slogans that omit useful product information and favors factual specificity.
Adaptation: State scope, mechanics, relevant limits and verifiable value, avoiding empty superiority promises.
Limit: Important facts depend on audience and medium; neither longer copy nor facts alone guarantee outcomes.

## OG-088 · Chapter 19 · What’s wrong with advertising?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The book defends advertising but raises concerns about deception, political marketing and media clutter.
Adaptation: Avoid unsupported guarantees, misleading urgency, fabricated endorsements and unjustified assumptions.
Limit: Do not treat the author’s dated statements about regulation or politics as current legal guidance.
