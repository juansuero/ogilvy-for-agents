# Selected source principles

Paraphrases of selected material from *Ogilvy on Advertising*. Historical studies and assertions are not independently verified. Adaptations and limits belong to this toolkit.

The book was published in 1983; digital workflows, statistical techniques, and modern industry practices here are explicit modern adaptations.

## OG-024 · Chapter 4 · PROFIT AND ALL THAT

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Insufficient service can drive clients away; excessive generosity can undermine viability. Size and profit differ.
Adaptation: Review scope, delivery costs and support obligations when evaluating a proposal.
Limit: Do not import historic agency margins or invent profitability inputs.

## OG-026 · Chapter 4 · HOW TO GET PAID

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The fee discussion covers paying for wanted services, account-level sustainability and the suspicion that commissions can create. He ultimately emphasizes reasonable profit rather than banning a payment model.
Adaptation: Make service scope and commercial incentives explicit; analyze each account rather than hiding losses across clients.
Limit: Not a prohibition on every commission and not a pricing formula.

## OG-027 · Chapter 4 · DISCIPLINE WORKS / WHAT TO DO WITH YOUR PROFITS

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author recommends written goals checked against accomplishment and a reserve for difficult periods, while warning about ventures outside competence.
Adaptation: Evaluate workload against outcomes and consider resilience when judging commitments.
Limit: Not tax, investment, medical or employment advice; historical numbers and all-night-work rhetoric are not runtime guidance.

## OG-031 · Chapter 5 · CREDIT RISKS and adjacent paragraphs

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Consider payment risk, compatible values, the expense of servicing promised future growth and relationships that damage morale.
Adaptation: Separate committed scope from hoped-for future work and make accept/renegotiate/decline reasoning explicit.
Limit: Do not equate negotiation or limited budgets with disrespect; do not convert the referral-fee anecdote into a universal ban.

## OG-032 · Chapter 5 · BIG AGENCIES VS. SMALL

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: A small agency can lose independence of judgment through fear of losing an oversized client; size has resource and bureaucracy tradeoffs.
Adaptation: Check actual delivery capacity and the ability to advise honestly under revenue concentration.
Limit: No fabricated safe concentration percentage or automatic instruction to hire.

## OG-075 · Chapter 16 · Don’t waste time on problem babies

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The author advises investing in winners rather than endlessly trying to revive failing products.
Adaptation: Compare prospective return, evidence, strategic constraints and opportunity costs.
Limit: Do not automatically abandon a product on one weak result or ignore necessary product fixes.

## OG-076 · Chapter 16 · Don’t dawdle

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy stresses that time matters economically and describes frequent progress review.
Adaptation: Define a bounded review cadence and decision triggers instead of indefinite debate.
Limit: A 30-day interval is a historical example, not a required schedule for every project.

## OG-086 · Chapter 19 · Can advertising sell bad products?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: Ogilvy argues strong promotion cannot reliably secure repeat purchases for a disappointing product; improving the product is often key.
Adaptation: Diagnose usage, fulfillment and retention before attributing weak sales solely to ads.
Limit: Some short-term sales from promotion remain possible; do not conclude defects without evidence.

## OG-088 · Chapter 19 · What’s wrong with advertising?

Locator: chapter and section in the heading.
Basis: author_recommendation
Source summary: The book defends advertising but raises concerns about deception, political marketing and media clutter.
Adaptation: Avoid unsupported guarantees, misleading urgency, fabricated endorsements and unjustified assumptions.
Limit: Do not treat the author’s dated statements about regulation or politics as current legal guidance.
